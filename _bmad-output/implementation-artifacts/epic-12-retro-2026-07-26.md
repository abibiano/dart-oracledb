## What Went Well

### "Measure, don't assume" paid off four separate times, with teeth

Every live-measurement claim in this epic was checked against both fixtures before it went into
code or docs, and it wasn't a formality — it closed a targeted ledger item as a non-issue,
surfaced two *unrelated* real leaks (the `IN OUT` OUT-locator leak, the ORA-22922
handle-consumption gap), and **overturned two of the epic's own documented claims** (12.3's "a
shrunk LOB fails loud" never materialized; a LOB locator is read-consistent and returns the OLD
value in full). This is the single highest-value habit in the epic and is now written into
`project-context.md` as a hard rule.

### Compile-time enumeration of a threaded flag found exactly what it promised

Making `fetchLobs` a `required` internal parameter (rather than defaulted) converted a silent
default-bug class into a compile error and found the 3 omitted call sites the reviewer predicted.
Worth extending to every future flag threaded through `connection.dart` — it's cheaper than a
checklist and it doesn't rely on anyone remembering to run it.

### The epic-boundary review earned its keep on a defect no per-story review could reach

Three lenses independently found that `_openResultSetGuarded` never threaded `fetchLobs` into
`_materializeNestedCursorsInBatch`, so streaming `resultSet: true + fetchLobs: true` skipped the
cursor-embedded-LOB reject entirely and fell into the exact ORA-12547 decode failure the epic had
measured as undecodable — on 21c, poisoning the transport. This defect only exists at the seam
between 12.3's streaming path and 12.3's own reject guard; no single story's review could have
seen it. This is the clearest evidence yet that a whole-epic integration pass is doing real,
irreplaceable work.

### Docs got the same adversarial treatment as code — on the second pass

Iteration 2's Medium findings were mostly about iteration 1's own documentation fixes shipping a
new *false* remedy claim ("`fetchLobs: true` is the safer way to meet the cursor boundary") in
four places, contradicting the epic's own measurement. The now-codified rule — a fix that adds a
remedy claim must cite the measurement row proving it reachable — came directly out of catching
this. Docs are now explicitly a review surface, not an afterthought.

### Exact counter assertions, not bounds, earned their keep repeatedly

`equals()` assertions on `debugCacheSize`/`debugPendingTempLobCount`/etc. corrected wrong mental
models multiple times this epic (a `SELECT ... FOR UPDATE` takes no cache slot at all; a repeated
LOB SELECT holds exactly one). The epic-boundary review flagged two remaining loose-bound
assertions as violations of this now-ratified rule and both were tightened.

## Challenges

### The epic-boundary review did not converge — and that's the headline, not a footnote

Two iterations, six lenses, two reviewer models, and iteration 2 produced its own fresh 18-item
findings list — nearly the same volume as iteration 1, with 3 of 4 Medium findings being defects
iteration 1's own fixes introduced. The auto-bmad epic cap stopped at 2 iterations with real
findings still surfacing, not with a clean pass. The honest read: this diff was not proven stable
under a third adversarial pass, and none was run. Shipping as a **draft PR** with stories held at
`review` is the correct, conservative response to that — not a workaround to make sprint-status
look prettier.

Abibiano (Project Lead): "What's the actual risk of merging as-is versus running a third pass?"

Amelia (Developer): "Iteration 2's own pattern — Medium findings being iteration 1's fixes'
side-effects — is the strongest argument for a third pass before this merges for real, even
though the cap says stop at two. I'd treat 'run a confirming third iteration, or have a human
review the draft PR line-by-line before merge' as the actual next action, not 'ship it.'"

### TEA disabled meant 12.4's own ACs were the epic's only exit gate

With `bmad-testarch-*` skills unavailable in BMAD 6.10.0, there was no trace matrix, no NFR
assessment, and no independent test-quality review layered on top of the per-story Acceptance
Auditor + security pass. 12.4's AC1/AC10 became the de-facto epic exit criteria by default, not by
design. This worked out — dual-env rigor and "measure, don't assume" carried real weight — but it
is a single point of failure: if 12.4 itself had a gap in its own ACs, nothing downstream would
have caught it.

### 12.4 absorbed scope a pure test-closeout story would not normally own

Story 12.4 ("dual-env integration tests") ended up also owning: the CHANGELOG/version-bump release
work 12.3 explicitly deferred to "the release/epic-closing story," AND a measured production leak
fix (the `IN OUT` OUT-locator leak) that surfaced only because 12.4 was doing the live measurement
work anyway. Both are defensible in this instance — with no TEA gate, 12.4 already *was* the
epic-closing story, and the leak fix piggybacked on evidence 12.4 collected regardless — but it
means one story carried three distinct kinds of risk (test coverage, release hygiene, and a
production defect fix) with only the review layers a normal story gets. Confirmed here, at retro:
this was the right call *for this epic*, but it should be named as a repeatable pattern — "the
epic-closing dual-env story also owns release artifacts and any defect the dual-env pass measures
true" — rather than re-decided ad hoc at every epic's equivalent story.

### Two cross-AC contradictions again reached review before being caught

12.2 shipped with AC4 ("fetched handles keep holding the in-flight slot") directly contradicting
AC5 ("a fetched handle is bindable") — unreachable as specified. 12.3 had a milder version (a
hold-back-and-join clause vs. a hard per-piece size cap) that was resolved cleanly in code but
still leaked a false bound into public dartdoc and README. This is the same class of miss Epic 11
didn't have and Epic 12 had twice — cross-AC contradiction detection needs to happen at
story-authoring time, not discovered during code review or (worse) shipped into user-facing docs.

### `architecture.md`'s "one lifecycle model" invariant no longer matches what shipped

The architecture's cross-cutting rule states `OracleResultSet`, REF cursors, and
streamed/temp LOBs "all share the same discipline: hold the connection's single in-flight slot for
their lifetime..." with no carve-out. 12.2 found this structurally incompatible with binding a
*created* temporary LOB and narrowed it — created-temp handles are exempt from the in-flight-slot
guard, documented correctly in `project-context.md`, but `architecture.md` itself was never
updated to state the exception. See Planning Drift below.

## Key Insights

1. **A capped, two-iteration whole-epic review is not the same thing as a converged one.** This
   epic is the proof: iteration 2 produced a fresh 18-item list, 3 of 4 Medium findings being
   defects iteration 1's own fixes introduced. If the cap is hit while findings are still
   non-trivial, the honest epic status is "draft, needs a confirming pass or a human review," not
   "done." Recommend treating a non-empty final-iteration findings list as a mandatory signal to
   route to human review before merge, distinct from the "0 High/Med, ship it" case.
2. **`required` internal parameters beat a checklist for flag-threading — but only where the flag
   is actually made required.** The one surviving flag-threading gap the epic-boundary review
   caught existed specifically on a call site where `fetchLobs` wasn't required. Audit every flag
   threaded through `connection.dart`/`transport.dart` and confirm each internal call site
   receiving it is `required`, not defaulted.
3. **Docs are a review surface with the same failure mode as code: a fix can regress on the next
   pass.** Iteration 1's docs fix introduced a user-facing falsehood iteration 2 had to catch. Any
   fix that adds a remedy claim needs the measurement citation in the same diff, not as a
   follow-up.
4. **Cross-AC contradictions are now a repeat pattern (Epic 11 didn't have one; Epic 12 had two).**
   Story-authoring needs an explicit "do these ACs agree with each other" pass, especially for
   lifecycle/concurrency stories where one AC narrows a guard and another assumes it's already
   loosened.
5. **A pre-story architecture confirmation review, when skipped, resurfaces as a mid-epic design
   tension.** Epic 11's action item asking for exactly this (item 4) was not done, and 12.2 hit the
   created-temp-LOB/slot-rule tension mid-story that the review was meant to catch early. Second
   data point (after Epic 10's csfrm model) that skipping this step costs a mid-flight AC
   amendment.
6. **TEA-shaped gaps are absorbable once, but they concentrate risk into whichever story happens to
   close the epic.** It worked this time because "measure, don't assume" and dual-env rigor are
   strong habits independent of TEA. That's a property of this team's discipline, not a property of
   the process — it should not be relied on silently epic after epic.

## Planning Drift

Comparing what Epic 12 actually built against the planning artifacts it started from:

1. **`architecture.md` — structural.** The cross-cutting invariant "One lifecycle model for all
   lazy, server-backed handles" (Epic 12 section, "In-flight ownership & lifecycle invariants")
   states `OracleResultSet`, REF cursors, and streamed/temp LOBs all hold the single in-flight slot
   for their lifetime with no exception. Epic 12 discovered this is **not** true for *created*
   temporary LOBs — they are deliberately exempted so they can be bound while open — and shipped
   that exception correctly, but the architecture document itself was never amended to record it.
   Recommend a targeted `architecture.md` update (not a full re-sync) adding the created-temp-LOB
   carve-out as a named exception to the invariant, so the next epic that reads this section
   doesn't inherit a false universal rule.
2. **`epics.md` — detail-level, recurring.** Epic 12's four stories were each a single summary line
   in `epics.md` ("12.1 Public LOB object — read/write streaming for CLOB/BLOB.", etc.); every
   real AC, scoping decision, and API-surface call was authored from scratch by `create-story`
   against the LOB source and the node-oracledb reference, not pre-specified. This is the same
   gap noted in Epic 11's retro and is structurally unchanged for Epics 13–15 (equally one-line
   stubs). Detail-level, not structural — the epic goals and boundaries themselves are still
   correct — but it recurs every epic and shifts real scoping risk onto `create-story` each time.
   Worth a light enrichment pass on Epics 13–15's stubs before their first stories are created,
   mirroring the fuller detail Epic 12's stories ended up needing anyway.

No PRD-level drift found: all NFRs and FRs referenced by Epic 12 (resource cleanup, fail-loud
decode, dual-platform/dual-version test mandate) still match what shipped.
